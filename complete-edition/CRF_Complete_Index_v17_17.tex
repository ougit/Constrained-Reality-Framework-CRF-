\begin{contentsbox}[title={Pre-Work Audit Protocol --- Quick Reference}]
\textbf{Step 1.} Identify your task's domain (geometry, gauge, mass,
thermodynamics, mind layer, \ldots).

\textbf{Step 2.} Scan the Contents Maps for Parts A--D below.
Locate the Part(s) and Section(s) most relevant.

\textbf{Step 3.} Read the corresponding ZC-paper abstract (Parts~C
and~D) to confirm what was established and what gaps remain open.

\textbf{Step 4.} Cross-reference the atomic-unit registry in the
relevant paper before introducing any new DEF/CONJ/FIND/THM.

\textbf{Rule:} Existing CRF objects take priority over new
constructions. Combine existing pillars before inventing.
\end{contentsbox}

\begin{contentsbox}[title={Scope of This Index --- Read Before Relying On It}]
This Index is a \textbf{navigation / triage layer}. It is sufficient to:
(a)~prevent duplication (know an object already exists);
(b)~run a Pre-Work Audit existence scan without opening source papers;
(c)~see the gap / PM / discovery topology of the whole programme.

\smallskip
It is \textbf{not} sufficient, on its own, to \emph{reuse exact prior
machinery} in a new derivation. A one-line registry entry tells you a
theorem exists; it does not always carry the exact statement, domain
conditions, or proof. The load-bearing exact forms are collected in the
Quick-Reference Pack below and flagged ``exact form in Index: Y''. For
anything not so flagged, the one-liner is a pointer, not the object.

\smallskip
\textbf{Domain-boundary caution (PWA-7).} A flat one-line description
strips the domain. The physics-layer spectral gap $\lambda_2$ and the
mind-layer $\lambda_1^{\rm \bar{a}sava}$ both read as ``a spectral
gap'' when compressed to one line --- they are \emph{not}
interchangeable. Re-confirm the domain in the source before any
cross-domain inheritance.
\end{contentsbox}

\begin{contentsbox}[title={Missing-Value Protocol --- Three-Tier Fallback (v17.13)}]
When a research value or exact form is needed and may not be in this
Index, resolve it in this order. \textbf{Never guess or reconstruct a
near-form} --- that is the precise origin of the ZC33 scar
(approximating $K^{*}\Tavg/F = e$ instead of using the exact identity).

\smallskip
\textbf{Tier 1 --- In Index.} The value / exact equation is in the
Quick-Reference Pack (constant table or load-bearing equation table,
flagged ``Y''). Use it directly; cite the source ID.

\smallskip
\textbf{Tier 2 --- Fetch one source paper.} Not in the Index, but the
session can open the source. Fetch the single source paper for the
object. \emph{Mandatory trigger:} if a new paper inherits from anything
\textbf{older than ZC64}, fetch that source --- the Index carries only
a one-liner for deep-inheritance objects (Parts~A--F machinery:
$\Tavg$, the TLS floor, K14, $\Gamma_{\rm CRF}$).

\smallskip
\textbf{Tier 3 --- Ask the user.} Not in the Index \emph{and} the
source cannot be opened (free-tier, context full). \textbf{Ask the user
for the exact value or form.} Do not proceed on an approximation. This
is the safe failure mode for a low-context session and protects
correctness over convenience.
\end{contentsbox}
